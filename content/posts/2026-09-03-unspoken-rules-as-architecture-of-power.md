The unspoken rules never needed the buildings to stay standing. In the law firm the partners still moved as if the corner offices conferred something permanent, even after the lease was renegotiated and the firm shrank by half. A junior could be asked to speak and then, after the first sentence, the senior litigator would set his pen down without looking up. The discussion ended there. No one wrote it down. The rule lived in the interval between the pen and the next breath.

The Eastern Shore house taught the same lesson more quietly. By the time I was old enough to notice, the money that had kept the place was already gone, yet the sequence of rooms still told guests where they belonged. The second-floor bedroom with the faded wallpaper was offered without explanation; the smaller one at the end of the hall was never mentioned. Sherry appeared at the same hour the light changed on the water. Topics that might have disturbed the surface were simply not raised. The roof leaked and the dock boards were soft underfoot, but the etiquette remained load-bearing. It outlasted the capital because it no longer required money to enforce itself.

I keep trying to photograph what remains when the visible authority has already left. A single coffee cup on the sill, the rim turned away from the light. A bed half-made, sheets pulled up on one side only, as though someone had been called away mid-gesture. These are not evidence of presence so much as evidence of the assumption that presence would continue. The camera records the pause, not the rule itself. That is the closest it can come.

What interests me now is how these same pauses operate once the institution has become personal rather than professional. The moment a name is withheld or offered, the chair left empty at the table even when the room is crowded, the conversation that stops just before the practical detail is exchanged. These are not failures of attention. They are the architecture still doing its work after the blueprints have been discarded. The photograph cannot name the rule, but it can hold the space where the rule was felt. That space is what I return to, because the rule itself has already moved on.
