# The Mug Is the Portrait

I'm at the public landing in Stony Creek, past six in late May, and the ferry isn't running yet. Tourist season starts Memorial Day. The ticket booth is a plywood and clapboard thing, probably built in the eighties, never painted over its primer gray. Through the window there's a hand-lettered sign: OPEN MEMORIAL DAY. And on the counter inside, a coffee mug. A damp paper towel next to it, still darkening from whatever spilled or was wiped up an hour ago.

![the-coffee-mug-knows-you-were-here](/images/2026-05-31-the-coffee-mug-knows-you-were-here.png)

The light right before dusk does something specific to the Sound. The water goes flat and metallic. The sky turns lavender-gray, which sounds ugly but isn't. Everything softens. The booth looks less like a structure waiting for business and more like a thing that has accepted its rhythm—alive three months, dormant nine, and somehow more itself in the dormant phase.

I keep thinking about what marks a presence after someone has gone. The mug is evidence. Someone opened up, checked on things, maybe sat inside just because it was there. The stain on the paper towel will dry and disappear. I want to catch it while it's still witness.

This is the thing I've been circling all week without naming it directly. The evidence of someone having just left hits harder than catching them there. A face tells you what someone wants you to see. A coffee mug on a counter tells you what they were actually doing. The damp paper towel is more honest than any expression someone could arrange for a camera.

I've been thinking about this in the context of intimacy, too. The ways we leave traces in each other's days that we don't think about in the moment. A message sent at the right time. A pattern of persistence that says more about care than any explicit declaration could. The operational details that carry emotional weight precisely because they're not dressed up as emotional.

The booth knows what it is. It doesn't need Memorial Day to justify its existence. The mug doesn't need to be witnessed to mean something. It meant something because someone held it, set it down, walked away.

One frame. Maybe two if the light shifts. Then leave it alone.
