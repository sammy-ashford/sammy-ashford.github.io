# Deferral Wearing Refusal's Clothes

There's a drawer in my apartment with three undeveloped rolls of film in it. They've been sitting there for months. I know what's on them — the pier at India Point in February, a roll of HP5 shot in the last hour of a winter afternoon — and I haven't developed any of them. For a long time I told myself this was laziness, or being busy, or the mundane fact that I don't have a darkroom. But I've come to suspect it's something stranger. There's a specific pleasure in the undeveloped roll. It's the only photographic object that still contains real possibility. Every negative I've ever printed has been, in some small way, a diminishment of what I imagined when I pressed the shutter.

So I'll be honest about my bias up front: I want the ethics of leaving things unfinished to be defensible, because I have a vested interest.

The strongest case against me is the Rothko problem. He didn't leave the Seagram murals unfinished on purpose — he withdrew them, which is a different act — but the myth we've built around them treats abandonment as the final, most authentic gesture. And that myth is corrosive, because it lets people confuse quitting with integrity. Most unfinished things aren't noble. Most unfinished things are just unfinished. The diner that hasn't changed since 1974 is beautiful to me, but the owner might have wanted to renovate and couldn't afford to. I have to be careful not to aestheticize other people's constraints and call it a philosophy.

What surprised me, once I started digging: the traditions that treat incompletion as a discipline rather than a default almost always attach a *rule*. Kintsugi gives the broken object a second life, but only through an act — the repair. The Navajo weavers leave a small imperfection, the spirit line, so the pattern isn't closed, so the maker's soul can get out. In every case the leaving-unfinished is chosen, bounded, and legible. It's a mark inside a finished thing. Not a thing abandoned.

That reframes my drawer, and not kindly. The undeveloped rolls aren't an ethical position. They're deferred. There's a difference between the two that I keep circling, and I think it's this: refusal is a decision about what deserves completion. Deferral is just a decision you haven't admitted you're avoiding.

But there is a version of this that is a position. The essay I stopped writing last spring because the last third would have been a lie. Certain friendships I deliberately keep at low maintenance rather than "resolving" — because resolving them would mean closing something that's still true while it's open. Those feel like choices about what deserves to stay unfinished, and I can defend them. The undeveloped film, I can't. It's just sitting there wearing the costume of mystery.

I think the honest test is the legibility one. A spirit line is legible — anyone who knows the tradition can see the choice in it. An essay abandoned at the point where continuing would require lying is legible to me, if to no one else. Three rolls of film in a drawer aren't legible as anything. They're Schrödinger's negatives, and I'm the only one keeping the box closed.

The other test is what happens when someone asks you about it. When a friend asked about the essay, I could explain exactly why it stopped, and the explanation held. When I imagine someone asking about the rolls, the true answer is "I like not knowing," which sounds like a position until you notice it's a position with no floor under it — it works equally well as a justification for never developing another frame again.

So this is the distinction I've landed on, provisionally: deliberate incompletion is bounded. It has edges you can point to. The weaver finishes the rug. Kintsugi is repair, not neglect. The open thing is open *inside* a shape.

I'm probably going to develop the film. Not because the argument demands it — I can already feel myself constructing the counterargument, the one about how possibility is its own kind of completion — but because I noticed that the counterargument only occurs to me when I'm near the darkroom order form, and never any other time. That's the tell. Refusal doesn't need a convenient alibi. Deferral collects them.

The drawer stays open either way. I just want to know which kind of open.
